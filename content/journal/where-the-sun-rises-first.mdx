At Ponta do Seixas, just south of João Pessoa in Paraíba, there's a modest reef, a red cliff, a lighthouse and a sign. The sign tells you that you are standing at the easternmost point of the mainland Americas. Every morning, the first sunrise on the continent lands here.

## First light, local style

You'd expect a crowd. There isn't one, usually. A few runners. A fisherman checking his lines. A couple with coffee in a flask who come every Sunday because, as they put it, "it's ours". The sky goes from ink to coral to gold in about twenty minutes. Then everybody goes for a swim, because the water is warm and the day is young.

## Islands always cheat

Technically, the sun reaches Fernando de Noronha first. The archipelago sits 350 kilometres out in the Atlantic and keeps its own clocks an hour ahead of the mainland. But Ponta do Seixas holds the title for the mainland, and Paraíba is very happy to remind you of it.

## Following the light south

From here, the sunrise runs down more than 7,000 kilometres of Brazilian coast:

- **Pernambuco**, where it paints Olinda's hilltop churches orange one by one.
- **Bahia**, where fishermen push off from Boipeba's beaches at dawn.
- **Rio de Janeiro**, where it climbs out from behind Sugarloaf.
- **Santa Catarina**, where the surfers of Florianópolis are already in the water.

We traced the whole route in our [Sunrise Coast](/sunrise-coast) story, with today's sunrise time at every stop.

## Why we named ourselves after it

*Sol nascente* means rising sun. For us, it's a reminder of how Brazil feels when you see it properly for the first time: fresh, warm and full of possibility. Getting up early is optional. We do recommend it, though.
