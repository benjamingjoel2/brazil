Every Brazilian has an opinion about the best Carnival, and every one of them is right. Here's our honest guide to choosing yours.

## Rio de Janeiro: the show

Rio's Carnival is two things at once. In the Sambódromo, the city's top samba schools each parade for over an hour: thousands of dancers, a drum section that hits you in the chest, and floats the size of apartment blocks. It is judged like a sport, and the city takes it just as seriously.

Outside, the **blocos** take over. These street parties start in the morning and range from tiny neighbourhood gatherings to crowds of hundreds of thousands.

**Choose Rio if** you want spectacle, you're happy with big crowds, and you don't mind paying for it. Book early: hotels sell week-long packages at premium prices.

## Salvador: the street party

Salvador's Carnival moves. *Trios elétricos* (trucks stacked with speakers, a band on top) roll slowly along the seafront and through the old centre, and the crowd moves with them. The Afro blocos, drum groups like Olodum and Ilê Aiyê, are its heart.

You can follow a trio inside its roped-off area with an *abadá* (a group T-shirt that works as a ticket), or watch from a *camarote*, an elevated lounge with food, drink and somewhere to sit down.

**Choose Salvador if** you want to dance, you love percussion, and you have stamina.

## Olinda and Recife: the whole town in costume

Olinda's Carnival is free, on foot and homemade. Giant puppets sway through cobbled lanes, brass bands play frevo at a sprint, and maracatu drums shake the church doors. Down the hill, Recife's Galo da Madrugada parade fills the city on Carnival Saturday.

**Choose Olinda if** you want joy over glamour, history over high-rises, and you've packed good shoes.

## Or skip it beautifully

Carnival week is a national holiday, and some of our favourite trips that week have no Carnival in them at all: a river lodge in the Amazon, a fazenda in the Pantanal, a quiet island in Bahia. Fly before Friday and back after Ash Wednesday to avoid the busiest travel days.

## A few honest tips

- Dates move every year with Easter, so check yours early.
- Book flights and rooms six to twelve months ahead for Rio and Salvador.
- Wear a costume. Nobody takes it seriously, which is exactly the point.
- Leave valuables at the hotel and carry a little cash in a front pocket.
- Plan a rest day. You will need it.
