Rio is a wonderful place to start. It is not a wonderful place to stop.

Brazil is bigger than the contiguous United States, with six biomes, twenty-six states and a different soundtrack in almost every one of them. A first trip that only sees Rio and a beach is a bit like visiting Europe and only seeing Paris and a sunlounger. Lovely. Incomplete.

## Think in regions, not cities

The trick to Brazil is to choose two or three regions that contrast with each other, and give each one time. A classic shape looks like this:

- **A city with a heartbeat**: Rio for mountains and beaches, or Salvador for music, food and Afro-Brazilian culture.
- **A wild place**: the Pantanal for jaguars and giant otters, the Amazon for rivers and canopy, or Bonito for rivers so clear you float above the fish.
- **Somewhere to slow down**: a car-free island in Bahia, the colonial lanes of Paraty, or the dunes and lagoons of Lençóis Maranhenses.

## Fly, and be honest about it

Distances here are real. Rio to Manaus is a four-hour flight, longer than London to Athens. We plan routes so that each flight earns its place, and we say so plainly: most of our journeys use two to four internal flights. In exchange, you get to wake up in a fazenda on the floodplain on Tuesday and swim off a sandbank in Bahia on Friday.

## Where we'd send a first-timer who's already seen Rio

- **Salvador and the Bahia coast.** The food alone is worth the flight: moqueca bubbling in a clay pot, acarajé hot from the dendê oil, cocadas for dessert. Add a few days on Boipeba or in Itacaré and your shoulders will drop an inch.
- **The Pantanal.** If you want wildlife, nowhere in South America is more generous. In the dry season, jaguars patrol the riverbanks; in the wet, the plains fill with birds.
- **Minas Gerais.** Baroque churches, cobbled hill towns, the world's most comforting cheese bread and Inhotim, a contemporary art park set in a botanical garden.
- **Lençóis Maranhenses.** White dunes to the horizon and, in the right months, freshwater lagoons between them. It looks like a trick of the light. It isn't.

## The secret ingredient

It's people. A boatman who knows where the otters den. A cook who won't give you the recipe but will give you seconds. A musician who insists you join in. Brazil opens up when someone local opens the door, and that's what we're for.

Start in Rio, by all means. Then let us show you the rest.
